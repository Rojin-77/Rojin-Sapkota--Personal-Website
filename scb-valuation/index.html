Valuation of Standard Chartered Bank Nepal
using Top-Down Approach

Project Overview

In this project, I performed a detailed valuation of Standard Chartered Bank Nepal (SCB) using a **top-down valuation methodology**, which starts from macroeconomic drivers and narrows down to bank-specific projections.

The analysis includes scenario modeling (base, optimistic, and pessimistic cases) and focuses on projecting Nepal's private sector lending growth in line with forecasted GDP growth. SCB’s potential market share is then estimated based on historical performance and competitive positioning.

This work demonstrates my approach to valuing commercial banks in Nepal, incorporating regulatory constraints from the Nepal Rastra Bank (NRB) capital adequacy framework, which directly impacts lending capacity through required capital ratios.

Key Assumptions & Insights

  • Competitive strengths: SCB maintains higher capital adequacy ratios and significantly lower non-performing loans (NPLs) compared to industry peers, giving it a structural advantage for growth.
  • Lending constraint: A high proportion of call / short-term deposits requires SCB to hold more liquid assets, resulting in a lower credit-to-deposit (CD) ratio and more conservative lending behavior.
  • Macro linkage: Private sector credit growth is modeled in alignment with Nepal’s expected real GDP growth , adjusted for inflation, monetary policy, and regulatory changes.
  • Scenario analysis: Different combinations of GDP growth, market share capture, and margin assumptions produce a valuation range, helping assess upside potential and downside risks.
