Valuation of Standard Chartered Bank Nepal

Top-Down Valuation Method with Scenario Analysis

Overview

This valuation analyzes Standard Chartered Bank Nepal (SCB) using a Top-Down valuation approach, linking macroeconomic growth to sectoral credit expansion and ultimately to the bank’s sustainable lending capacity.

Valuation Methodology

The analysis begins with projections of Nepal’s GDP growth and its impact on private sector lending. Based on these projections, SCB’s future loan book is estimated by assessing its market share within private sector credit.

The model incorporates Nepal Rastra Bank (NRB) regulatory constraints, including capital adequacy requirements, liquidity provisions, and credit-to-deposit (CD) ratio limits, ensuring that lending growth remains realistic and compliant.

Key Assumptions

  • SCB maintains a competitive advantage due to higher capital adequacy ratios.
  • Lower non-performing loan (NPL) levels support stable asset quality.
  • Prudent risk management enables sustainable profitability.
  • Lending capacity is constrained by a high proportion of call accounts in deposits.
  • Higher liquidity requirements reduce the achievable credit-to-deposit (CD) ratio.

Scenario Analysis

Multiple scenarios were evaluated by varying GDP growth assumptions, private sector credit expansion, and SCB’s market share. This allows assessment of valuation sensitivity under different economic environments.

Note: This valuation is a sample representation of my broader banking sector valuation work, which applies multiple banking ratios and regulatory frameworks to assess intrinsic value and sustainable growth.
